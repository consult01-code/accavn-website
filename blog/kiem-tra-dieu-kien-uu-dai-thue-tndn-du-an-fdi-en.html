CIT Incentives for FDI Projects: How to Check Eligibility

A self-check list for CIT incentive conditions of FDI projects, how to account separately for incentivized income, and mistakes that forfeit incentives.

Many investors assume incentives are "automatic" once the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký đầu tư) states that the project is eligible. In reality, corporate income tax incentives for investment projects are self-determined by the company at tax finalization and can be disallowed if the conditions cannot be proven. This guide explains how to self-check against four groups of conditions; it does not replace project-specific advice. Prepared by the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. General principles of CIT incentives for investment projects

The standard corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) rate is 20%. Under the 2025 Law on Corporate Income Tax (67/2025/QH15), enterprises with total annual revenue not exceeding VND 3 billion apply 15%, and those above VND 3 billion up to VND 50 billion apply 17%. Beyond these rates, investment projects may enjoy preferential rates and tax exemption/reduction periods if they meet the statutory conditions.

Three principles to remember:

  • The company self-determines its eligibility and self-declares the incentive at finalization; the tax authority checks afterwards.
  • Incentives attach to income from the qualifying project, and do not automatically apply to all of the company's income.
  • The new law has transitional provisions: projects already enjoying incentives under the old rules need to check how those incentives continue.

2. Four groups of conditions to self-check

Specific thresholds (capital levels, headcount, lists of areas) may have changed under the new law, so check the current legislation before drawing conclusions. If the project is in an area where administrative units have just been reorganized, review the transitional rules on incentivized locations.

3. Tracking incentivized income separately

This is what decides whether the incentive "holds" under inspection. The company must account separately for income from incentivized activities and non-incentivized income. Certain other income (such as deposit interest, foreign exchange differences and asset disposals) is usually not eligible; check the specific rules.

FAQ

If the IRC states the project is incentivized, is the incentive guaranteed?

No. The company must still self-determine and prove the conditions under the CIT law at finalization; the tax authority may re-examine them.

Is bank deposit interest eligible for the incentive?

Usually not, because it is other income outside the project's activities. Check the current rules for each item.

Can an expansion project receive its own incentive?

Possibly, if it meets the conditions and the additional income can be accounted for separately. If it cannot be separated, the allocation method set out in the regulations may be used.

Does the 2025 CIT Law affect incentives already being enjoyed?

The new law contains transitional provisions for projects already enjoying incentives. Companies should review their position before the 2026 finalization.
