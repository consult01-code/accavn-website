Compulsory Social Insurance for Foreign Employees in Vietnam

A checklist to determine whether an expat is subject to compulsory social insurance, the exclusions, and the impact on payroll costs.

Many FDI companies only discover gaps in social insurance for foreign employees when the social insurance authority inspects them or the auditor asks about expat salary costs. The right question is not "do expats have to contribute?", but "is this specific person covered, and which documents prove it?". Below is how the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam reviews each expat file.

1. Social insurance for foreign employees: the general principle

Under current regulations, foreign employees working in Vietnam under an employment contract and holding a work permit (or a practising certificate or practising licence) are subject to compulsory social insurance (BHXH – bảo hiểm xã hội), except in excluded cases. The conditions on contract term and on which insurance schemes apply to foreign employees should be checked against the latest regulations, as the Law on Social Insurance has been amended and the guidance may change.

The difference from Vietnamese employees is that not every insurance scheme applies in the same way. For example, unemployment insurance is subject to its own conditions, so the company should check before declaring rates on the system. Do not simply copy the payroll setup for Vietnamese staff to expats.

3. Exclusions that need careful checking

The three most common exclusions in FDI companies are intra-corporate transfers, employees who have reached retirement age and the application of an international treaty. The risk is that the company assumes an exclusion applies but does not keep supporting documents.

An assignment letter does not automatically make an expat an intra-corporate transferee. The basis stated on the work permit, and who actually manages and pays the person, are the key factors. See also work permits for foreign employees.

6. Common mistakes and how to avoid them

  • Assuming expats do not contribute because "the parent company already pays abroad", without an agreement or a certificate.
  • Forgetting to deregister when the work permit expires or the expat returns home, so contributions keep falling due.
  • Using the wrong contribution base: omitting allowances of a salary nature, or including benefits that are not part of the salary subject to social insurance.
  • Not aligning with PIT: the employee's compulsory social insurance contributions are deducted when calculating assessable income, so an insurance error leads to a tax error.

FAQ

Does an expat on a short-term contract have to pay social insurance?

The minimum contract term is set by current regulations and has changed under the amended Law on Social Insurance. The company should compare the actual contract term with the latest regulations before deciding not to contribute.

What documents should an intra-corporate transferee keep?

Keep the work permit stating the intra-corporate transfer basis, the assignment letter and the parent company's confirmation of the employment relationship. These are the main evidence when the social insurance authority inspects.

Are the employer's social insurance contributions for an expat deductible?

Compulsory insurance contributions made in accordance with the regulations and supported by documents are usually deductible for corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN). Contributions for ineligible persons or above the statutory levels need to be considered separately.
