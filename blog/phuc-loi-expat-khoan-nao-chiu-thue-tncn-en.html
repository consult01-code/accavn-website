Expat Housing, School Fees, Flights: Which Are Taxable in Vietnam?

A quick reference for common expat benefits: which are usually subject to personal income tax, and which depend on conditions and documents.

A foreign expert's package rarely consists of salary alone. Housing, children's school fees, home-leave flights and international health insurance often make up a significant share. Correctly identifying which expat benefits are subject to personal income tax (PIT/TNCN) helps the company avoid under-withholding and avoids expats paying tax they do not owe. This article provides an item-by-item reference table compiled by the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. General principle: cash and non-cash benefits can both be taxable

Income from salaries and wages includes non-cash benefits the employee receives, such as rent, school fees or membership fees paid by the company. Exceptions apply only to items that the PIT regulations expressly list as excluded from taxable income, subject to specific conditions.

The right approach is therefore not to memorize a list of "exempt items", but for each item to find the corresponding provision in the current regulations, check the conditions and keep supporting documents. The PIT regulations are being amended, so the table below only shows the usual treatment, to help you know what to look up.

2. Reference table: common expat benefits and PIT

For items that are "in some cases excluded", the conditions are usually in the details: who the beneficiary is, whether the payment is stated in the employment contract, and whose name is on the invoice. If one condition is missing, the item reverts to taxable income.

3. The two largest items: housing and school fees

Housing

Rent paid by the company on behalf of an expat is included in taxable income, but not above a percentage of total taxable income set by current regulations. The accountant needs to calculate the cap each month, compare it with the actual rent and take the lower figure. If the contract states a net salary, the housing amount included in income must also be grossed up, as explained in grossing up net salaries.

Children's school fees

The guidance contains provisions on school fees paid by the employer for children of foreign nationals studying in Vietnam at certain levels of education. Check the level of education, the place of study and how the documents are prepared. University fees, fees for study abroad or cash paid to the expat to pay the fees themselves usually need to be considered separately.

4. Process for checking which expat benefits are subject to PIT

Do not use caps or lists of exempt items copied from old articles. Some guidance on taxable income has changed; check the effective date of the regulation or ask the tax authority when large amounts are involved.

5. Documents and deductible expenses for the company

Expat benefits also affect corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN). For a payment to be a deductible expense, the company must state the benefit clearly in the employment contract or internal policy and hold valid invoices and documents. Invoices of VND 5 million or more (including VAT) must be paid by non-cash means for the input VAT to be deductible.

FAQ

If the company pays rent directly to the landlord, is the expat still taxed?

It may still be included in taxable income, within the statutory cap. Paying the landlord directly keeps the documentation clear, but does not automatically make the item exempt.

Are benefits provided to all employees subject to PIT?

General expenses where no named beneficiary can be identified are usually treated differently from expenses specific to an expat. Review the internal policy and how the documents are prepared.

Must benefits be included in income for a non-resident expat?

Yes. Benefits linked to work in Vietnam are still income arising in Vietnam and are taxed using the method that applies to non-resident individuals.
