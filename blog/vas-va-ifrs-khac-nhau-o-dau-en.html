VAS vs IFRS: 12 Key Differences FDI Accountants Should Know

A 12-point comparison of VAS and IFRS, an example of the impact on the numbers, and notes on the IFRS adoption roadmap in Vietnam.

FDI companies keep their books under Vietnamese Accounting Standards, but the parent company reads the numbers under IFRS. Understanding how VAS and IFRS differ lets you anticipate which adjustments will arise when preparing group reporting, rather than waiting for the group auditor to ask. Below are the 12 differences the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam encounters most often.

1. Why VAS and IFRS take different approaches

Vietnamese Accounting Standards (VAS – Chuẩn mực kế toán Việt Nam) were largely built on international standards in the early 2000s and have rarely been updated; they come with an accounting regime that prescribes the chart of accounts and report templates in detail. IFRS, by contrast, is continuously updated, is principles-based, relies on fair value and requires more judgment. The differences therefore lie not only in the numbers but also in presentation and disclosures.

3. Example of the impact on the numbers: an office lease

Suppose a company leases an office for 5 years at VND 1 billion per year (approximately USD 39,000 at a reference rate of VND 25,500/USD), paid at year-end, with an assumed discount rate of 5% per year. Under VAS this is an operating lease: VND 1 billion is expensed each year and nothing appears on the balance sheet. Under IFRS 16, the company recognizes a right-of-use asset and a lease liability equal to the present value of the 5 payments, about VND 4.33 billion.

The consequences: EBITDA is higher under IFRS (because rent becomes depreciation and interest), liabilities increase, and expenses are front-loaded into the early years. Groups with credit covenants tied to leverage ratios need to know this early.

5. The IFRS adoption roadmap in Vietnam

The Ministry of Finance has approved a scheme to apply International Financial Reporting Standards in two phases: voluntary adoption for certain groups of companies (including FDI companies whose parent requires it), followed by mandatory adoption for certain entities. The scope and timing of mandatory adoption should be checked against the latest regulations. For most FDI companies today, the common practice is still to keep the books under VAS and prepare a conversion schedule for the group; see preparing a reporting package for the parent company.

FAQ

Can an FDI company keep its books under IFRS instead of VAS?

Companies in Vietnam must still follow the Vietnamese accounting regime unless they are eligible for and register to apply IFRS under the Ministry of Finance roadmap. Check the latest regulations before deciding.

Which VAS–IFRS differences have the biggest impact on FDI companies?

Typically leases, allowances for receivables, deferred tax and prepaid expenses. The extent of the impact depends on each company's industry and asset structure.

Is corporate income tax calculated under VAS or IFRS?

Corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) in Vietnam is determined from accounting data under the Vietnamese regime and the tax regulations. IFRS adjustments serve group reporting and do not change the tax payable.
