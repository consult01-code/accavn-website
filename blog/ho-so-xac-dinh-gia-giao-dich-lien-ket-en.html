Transfer Pricing Documentation in Vietnam: When and What

The three-tier structure of transfer pricing documentation, how to check exemption yourself and a preparation roadmap for FDI companies.

When the tax authority audits a company that transacts with its parent, the first question is often: "Does the company have transfer pricing documentation (hồ sơ xác định giá giao dịch liên kết)?" This documentation is the evidence that intra-group prices are in line with the market. This article explains its structure, how to check whether you qualify for an exemption and how to prepare on a roadmap. It was compiled by the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. What is transfer pricing documentation?

It is the set of documents a company prepares and keeps to prove that the prices of its related party transactions comply with the arm's length principle. Unlike the disclosure appendix filed with the corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) finalization, transfer pricing documentation is usually kept at the company and presented when the tax authority requests it, within the time limit set by current regulations.

International practice (following OECD guidance) and Vietnamese regulations both divide the documentation into three tiers. Each tier answers a different question.

2. What does the local file contain?

For most small and medium-sized FDI companies, the local file is the part for which the company itself bears primary responsibility. A typical structure:

  1. Company information: management structure, organization chart, business activities, strategy.
  2. Related party transaction information: a description of each type of transaction, its value and contract terms.
  3. Functional analysis: what functions the company performs, what assets it uses and what risks it bears.
  4. Selection of the pricing method: the reasons for choosing the method and the independent comparables.
  5. Comparability analysis: data on comparable independent companies, the range and the conclusion.
  6. Financial information: financial statements and a profit split between related and independent activities.

3. How to check whether you are exempt from preparing documentation

Current regulations allow some companies to be exempt from preparing transfer pricing documentation, based on criteria such as revenue size, the total value of related party transactions, or the profit-to-revenue margin by sector. The specific thresholds may change, so rather than quoting figures here, we explain how to check for yourself.

Being exempt from documentation does not mean being exempt from the arm's length principle. The company must still file the related party transaction appendix, and prices can still be adjusted during a tax audit.

FAQ

Must transfer pricing documentation be filed with the finalization?

Usually the documentation is prepared, kept at the company and provided when the tax authority requests it; what is filed with the finalization is the disclosure appendix. Check the current rules on when it must be prepared and provided.

Does the subsidiary have to prepare the master file itself?

Usually the parent company prepares the master file and the subsidiary receives a copy to provide on request. The company should ask the parent to send an updated version each year.

Can transfer pricing documentation be written in English?

Check the language requirements under current regulations; in practice, the company should prepare a Vietnamese version or translation when providing it to the tax authority.
