How Much Charter Capital for an FDI Company in Vietnam?

How to budget charter capital for a foreign-owned company from actual operating costs, with a sample table and common risks.

“How much capital should we register?” is the first question most investors ask when preparing an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký đầu tư) application. There is no single right figure for every case: the charter capital of a foreign-owned company should be worked out from the actual spending plan, the requirements of the business line and the ability to contribute on time. This article explains how to build the budget step by step, with a sample table, prepared by the FDI accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. Why there is no single capital figure for every FDI company

Charter capital is the amount the members commit to contribute, as stated in the company charter. For foreign investors, this figure is also linked to the registered investment capital on the IRC. Licensing authorities, banks and partners all look at it to assess the project's financial capacity.

A two-person consulting firm and a manufacturing workshop have completely different capital needs. Some business lines have capital conditions under sector-specific regulations, which need to be checked separately. The right approach is therefore to work up from costs to the figure, not down from a number you have “heard about”.

5. Practical tips when settling the capital figure

  • Budget by month, not by year, to see clearly which month cash flow is most negative.
  • Separate charter capital from borrowings: fund long-term costs with equity and consider loans only for short-term needs.
  • Include compliance costs in the budget from the start: accounting, audit, insurance and personal income tax (PIT/TNCN).
  • Keep the budget as supporting evidence for the IRC application and when the bank asks about the purpose of the capital transfer.

FAQ

Is there a general minimum charter capital for FDI companies?

There is no single minimum for all sectors; some business lines have specific capital conditions under sector regulations. The licensing authority will consider whether the capital is appropriate to the scale of the project.

How long do we have to contribute charter capital?

For an FDI company, capital must be transferred through the direct investment capital account (DICA) and fully contributed within 90 days of the ERC issue date.

Can we register low capital and increase it later?

Yes, but each increase usually requires amending the IRC and ERC and updating the bank. Budget for at least 12 months to keep procedures to a minimum.
