Repatriating Profits with Accumulated Losses: A Worked Example

A hypothetical 3-year case showing when an FDI company with accumulated losses can still distribute profits, and the maximum it can distribute.

Many parent companies ask the same question: our Vietnamese subsidiary made a profit this year, so why does the accountant say profits cannot be remitted yet? The answer usually lies in accumulated losses: profits can be repatriated only once past losses have been fully offset. This article uses a hypothetical three-year case to explain how accumulated losses affect profit repatriation (and how much can be remitted while losses remain), with a calculation you can apply right away. Prepared by the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. What are accumulated losses and where do they appear in the financial statements?

Accumulated losses are a negative balance of undistributed profit after tax (retained earnings) on the balance sheet. The balance is the cumulative total of each year's after-tax results, less amounts already distributed. One profitable year does not necessarily wipe out the losses of previous years.

Under the rules on remitting profits abroad, an investor may repatriate profits only when the company has fulfilled its financial obligations and the distribution does not leave the company with accumulated losses. The deciding figure is therefore not this year's profit but the retained earnings balance after past losses have been offset. For the full procedure and documentation, see profit repatriation for FDI companies.

In 2025 the company made a profit before tax of VND 3 billion (≈ USD 118,000, approximate, at a reference rate of VND 25,500/USD), yet the maximum it can distribute is only about VND 800 million. The rest of the 2025 profit went to offset the VND 2 billion of accumulated losses from the first two years.

3. Three year-3 profit scenarios and the distributable amount

Keeping the first two loss-making years unchanged (VND 2 billion in total), we vary the 2025 profit before tax to see how the repatriation threshold shifts.

  • Profit of VND 1.5 billion: taxable income after loss carry-forward is still negative, so no tax is payable; retained earnings at year-end are still minus VND 500 million, so nothing can be remitted.
  • Profit of VND 2.2 billion: taxable income of VND 200 million, tax of VND 40 million; retained earnings are positive at VND 160 million.
  • Profit of VND 3 billion: as in the main case, a maximum of about VND 800 million.

4. How to calculate accumulated losses yourself before repatriating profits

Audit adjustments can turn a small profit into a loss. Do not send a profit distribution proposal to the parent company before the official audit report is issued.

5. How can you clear accumulated losses sooner?

There is no way to "get around" accumulated losses, but a company can avoid deepening them and plan distributions realistically:

  • Review accruals and provisions without a proper basis in loss-making years so the financial statements are accurate.
  • Track tax losses separately from accounting losses so you don't miss the right to carry losses forward.
  • Prepare a 2–3 year forecast for the parent company showing the year in which retained earnings are expected to turn positive.
  • Consider other financing arrangements with the parent company (for example, legitimate service fee payments), and check the related-party transaction rules carefully before applying them.

FAQ

We made a profit this year but still have accumulated losses. Can we remit part of it?

Only retained earnings that remain positive after all past losses are offset can be considered for distribution. If the balance is still negative after offsetting losses, nothing can be remitted yet.

Once tax losses are fully used, are the accounting accumulated losses also cleared?

Not necessarily. The two figures are calculated on different bases; non-deductible expenses or timing differences can make them diverge.

Can we increase capital to clear accumulated losses and then distribute profits?

A capital increase raises owner's equity but does not eliminate negative retained earnings. Any capital restructuring requires separate advice under current regulations.
