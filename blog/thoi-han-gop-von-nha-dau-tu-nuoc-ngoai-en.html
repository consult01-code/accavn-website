Capital Contribution Deadline for Foreign Investors in Vietnam

The 90-day charter capital timeline for FDI companies, scenarios for late or partial contribution, and how to track capital in the books.

The capital contribution clock starts ticking the moment you receive the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký doanh nghiệp). The charter capital contribution deadline for an FDI company is 90 days from the ERC issue date, and the capital must be transferred through the direct investment capital account (DICA). Many investors, busy opening accounts, applying for work permits and renting offices, notice the deadline only when it is too late. This article provides a tracking timeline, late-contribution scenarios and bookkeeping guidance, drawn from the FDI accounting practice of ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. When does the FDI capital contribution deadline start?

For a company established by a foreign investor, the starting point is the issue date of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC), not the issue date of the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký đầu tư) or the date the account is opened. Within 90 days, the investor must contribute the full committed capital, transferred through the company's DICA.

Because the ERC is usually issued within 3 working days of obtaining the IRC with a valid application, the gap between the two certificates is very short. Investors should therefore arrange the funds and bank procedures as soon as they file the IRC application.

3. Late or partial contribution scenarios and their consequences

Adjusting capital usually also requires an IRC amendment, so a shortfall is not just a bookkeeping issue but another round of administrative filings.

Impact on interest expense

If charter capital has not been fully contributed and the company borrows to cover the gap, under current regulations the interest expense corresponding to the unpaid capital may not be deductible for corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) purposes. The impact varies from case to case; have your accountant calculate it before deciding to borrow.

4. How to track contributed capital in the books

The accountant should keep a detailed owner's equity ledger for each investor and track three figures in parallel: capital committed under the ERC, capital received through the DICA and capital still to be contributed. For contributions in foreign currency, record each receipt at the bank's actual exchange rate on the date received.

Illustrative example: an investor commits USD 200,000 and transfers it in two tranches of USD 120,000 and USD 80,000 on different dates. The accountant records each tranche at the bank's rate on the date received, so the total VND capital in the books may differ from the estimated conversion used in the license application. This difference should be explained in the notes to the financial statements and discussed early with the auditor to avoid it being mistaken for a shortfall.

5. Three mistakes to avoid

  1. Counting from the IRC issue date or the account opening date, leading to the wrong 90-day deadline.
  2. Mixing capital contributions with funds to cover expenses in a single payment, so the capital in the books doesn't match the DICA statements.
  3. Not keeping the auditor informed of the contribution status, delaying the first-year audit report.

FAQ

What is the charter capital contribution deadline for an FDI company?

Capital must be fully contributed within 90 days of the ERC issue date and transferred through the company's direct investment capital account (DICA).

What should we do if capital is not fully contributed on time?

The company must register an adjustment of charter capital to the amount actually contributed within the prescribed time and consider amending the IRC. Seek advice before transferring further funds.

Does the parent company paying the office rent count as a capital contribution?

No. Capital must be transferred through the DICA for the correct purpose. Expenses paid by the parent company should be recorded as a payable or handled under a separate agreement.
