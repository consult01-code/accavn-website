Switching Accounting Providers: Handover Checklist for FDI Firms

An accounting handover checklist for changing providers: data, documents, e-accounts, open items and a safe cut-over date.

Changing accounting providers is not difficult; the hard part is not letting anything fall through the cracks: a return not filed, a provisional CIT payment forgotten, or the e-tax account password still held by the old provider. This article sets out an accounting handover process for FDI companies around a cut-over date, compiled by the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam from our experience onboarding clients.

1. Choosing the right cut-over date

The cut-over date is the day from which the new provider takes full responsibility. Choose a date that coincides with a month-end or quarter-end whose books are already closed, and avoid peak periods such as March (the deadline for corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) and personal income tax (PIT/TNCN) finalization and the annual financial statements is the last day of the third month after the financial year end, i.e. 31 March for a calendar year).

2. A complete accounting handover checklist

E-accounts and digital signatures

This is the part most easily missed. The e-tax, e-invoice, e-social insurance and e-banking accounts and the digital signature belong to the company, not to the provider. At handover, the company should change the passwords itself, revoke the old provider's access and grant the new provider access on a least-privilege basis. Passwords should not be sent by email or ordinary messaging.

4. The risk of filing gaps and what it costs

The biggest risk is a return falling into the gap between the two providers. Under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP (Nghị định 125/2020/NĐ-CP) and its amending documents, penalties on organizations for late filing of tax returns increase with the number of days overdue, on top of late-payment interest of 0.03% per day if tax is paid late.

Prevention: state clearly in the handover minutes the last filing period handled by the old provider and the first period handled by the new provider. See also penalties for late filing of tax returns.

Do not terminate the contract with the old provider before receiving the software data and the list of open items. Once the relationship has ended, requests for additional information are usually much slower and harder.

FAQ

What if the old provider refuses to hand over the data?

Check the handover clause in the contract and send a written request. In parallel, the company can still look up its returns and invoices on its own e-systems to rebuild the figures.

Is the new provider responsible for errors in prior periods?

Normally not, unless the contract includes a review and adjustment arrangement. Ask the new provider to review the opening balances and record its findings in writing.

Should we change providers during the audit season?

It is not advisable. If you must, agree that the old provider will complete the audit file for that year.

How long does a handover take?

For a small FDI company, typically about 4–6 weeks from notification until the new provider closes the first month, depending on data quality.
