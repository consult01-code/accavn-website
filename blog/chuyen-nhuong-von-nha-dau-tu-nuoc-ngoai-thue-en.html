Capital Transfers by Foreign Investors: Tax and Who Files

A hypothetical transfer of a capital contribution: calculating the gain, identifying the filing party and the order of procedures.

When a foreign investor sells its capital contribution in a Vietnamese company, the accountant's first questions are usually: who must file the tax, where, and on what amount? Tax on capital transfers by foreign investors is closely tied to the change-of-members file, the DICA and a cost basis recorded years earlier. ACCA Consulting Vietnam walks through a hypothetical case so you can see each step.

1. Who files tax on a foreign investor's capital transfer?

A foreign corporate seller usually has no tax code and no operations in Vietnam. Current regulations therefore assign responsibility for filing and paying on its behalf to a party in Vietnam, most commonly the Vietnamese company in which the foreign investor holds capital (the company whose capital is transferred), or the buyer if it is a Vietnamese organization, depending on the case. Before signing the contract, the parties should clearly agree who will file and record this in the contract.

Because the Vietnamese company may have to file for a transaction in which it receives no money directly, the accountant should ask the seller early for the contract, cost basis documents and payment documents.

2. Worked example: calculating the gain on transfer

Illustrative example: Parent company A (foreign) contributed VND 8 billion to Company X in Ho Chi Minh City, holding 40%. This year A sells its entire stake to Company B for VND 12 billion (approximately USD 470,600 at a reference rate of VND 25,500/USD). Directly related advisory and legal costs are VND 300 million.

The method for determining the tax payable when a foreign organization transfers capital (whether on the gain or on the transfer proceeds, and the applicable rate) has been revised under the 2025 Law on Corporate Income Tax (CIT/TNDN) and its implementing guidance. This article therefore does not give an indicative tax figure; before signing the contract, both parties should ask an adviser to calculate it under the rules in force at the time of transfer.

The cost basis is a frequent point of dispute

  • The cost basis must be documented: DICA statements for the capital transfer, the capital contribution certificate, and any earlier contract for purchasing the stake.
  • Capital contributed in foreign currency: agree how it is converted into VND in accordance with the regulations, rather than using an arbitrary exchange rate.
  • Transfer costs must be directly related and supported by valid invoices and documents.

3. Order of procedures when a foreign investor transfers capital

In many cases the registration authority requires or refers to the completion of tax obligations when processing a change of members. See amending the IRC to learn when both certificates must be amended.

4. Common risks and how to avoid them

If the transfer price is abnormally low, the tax authority may impose a deemed price. Where the Vietnamese company is the filing party and fails to file, the risk of penalties and late-payment interest may fall on the company itself.

FAQ

Does the Vietnamese company have to file on the seller's behalf if it receives no money?

It may. Where both parties are overseas, the regulations usually assign the filing to the Vietnamese company whose capital is transferred. Check in advance and ask the parties to provide the documents.

Do we need to file if the transfer is at a loss?

It is still advisable to file so there is a record showing that no tax arises, especially when the licences need to be updated. A low transfer price must also be explainable.

Can a tax treaty be used to obtain an exemption?

Some treaties contain provisions on income from the alienation of property. Applying them requires a notification file as prescribed and a case-by-case review of each treaty.
