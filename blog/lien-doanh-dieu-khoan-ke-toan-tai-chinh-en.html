A checklist of 9 financial clauses in a joint venture agreement and the accounting consequences of each.

Most joint venture disputes do not start with strategy but with numbers: how much the contributed assets are worth, when profits are distributed, who may see the books. A joint venture agreement with carefully drafted financial terms can spare both parties years of argument. This article lists 9 clauses to agree early and explains the accounting consequences of each, from the perspective of the FDI accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam (it does not replace legal advice from a lawyer).

1. Why the financial terms of a JV agreement are often overlooked

During negotiations, the parties usually focus on the market, the brand and management rights. Accounting wording gets pushed into an appendix or left “to be agreed later”. When the company makes a profit or a loss, each party interprets things differently. The accountant then has no basis for recording transactions, and the auditor will struggle to form an opinion if the policies are unclear.

4. Illustrative example: profit sharing by capital ratio

Illustrative example: a joint venture with total annual revenue above VND 50 billion applies the standard corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) rate of 20%. Profit before tax for 2026 is VND 5 billion (≈ USD 196,000, approximate, at a reference rate of VND 25,500/USD), assuming no tax adjustments. CIT is VND 1 billion and profit after tax VND 4 billion. The foreign investor holds 60% and the Vietnamese partner 40%.

The example shows why the profit-sharing clause must be explicit: whether distributions are based on the year's profit after tax or on accumulated profit, whether funds are appropriated first, and that profits are distributed only once the tax finalization is complete. Otherwise, the party paid first may have to refund money if the audited figures change. Remitting profits abroad has its own procedure; see profit repatriation for FDI companies.

5. Deadlock and exit: preparing for the worst

Deadlock usually arises over three decisions: budget approval, borrowing and profit distribution. The agreement should set value thresholds requiring unanimous consent, a mediation period and a deadlock-breaking mechanism, such as appointing an independent expert or a buy-sell option over the stake.

The exit clause should define how the stake will be valued from the outset (book value, fair value determined by an independent party, etc.). Divestment triggers registration amendments and capital transfer tax obligations, so clean books allow a quick valuation.

  2. Schedule quarterly reconciliations of contributed capital and balances between the parties.
  3. Appoint the independent audit firm early, before the end of the first financial year.

FAQ

What accounting points apply to in-kind contributions in a JV?

You need valuation minutes accepted by the parties and documents transferring ownership. The recognized value is the basis for depreciation, deductible expenses and ownership ratios, so it must be clear from the start.

Should a JV agreement name the audit firm?

It is better to set out the selection process and report deadline rather than hard-code a firm's name. Foreign-invested enterprises must have their annual financial statements audited, so this clause avoids disputes every year-end closing season.

What if the Vietnamese party contributes capital late?

The agreement should state the consequences: adjusting ownership ratios, allowing the other party to contribute instead, or reducing charter capital. The accountant must track each party's actual contributed capital to record it correctly.

Who should keep the books for a joint venture?

Either an in-house accountant or an independent accounting firm. What matters is that the document approval process and access to the books are agreed in the contract.
