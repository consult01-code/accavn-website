When the group requires you to compare several providers, a clear Request for Proposal (RFP) makes the quotes comparable. Without information on volumes or group reporting, each bidder will price a different scope and you will not know which is the real price. This article sets out an RFP framework for outsourcing accounting services for an FDI company, with a scorecard, prepared by ACCA Consulting Vietnam from real requests we have received.

1. Why use an RFP when outsourcing accounting for an FDI company?

Accounting fees depend mainly on document volume, headcount, entity type and reporting requirements. If the RFP does not state these factors, providers have to guess. A low quote may cover only tax filings, while a high quote may already include monthly group reporting. A good RFP helps you:

  • Compare quotes on the same scope of work.
  • Spot early any provider that cannot meet your language, system or reporting calendar requirements.
  • Have ready-made content for the contract and the SLA appendix later on.

3. Describing volumes so quotes reflect reality

Use the average of the last 3–6 months, not a peak or a trough month. For a newly established company, give estimates with the expected changes (for example, production starting in Q2). FAQ

Do I need to send financial statements to providers when requesting quotes?

Not necessarily. Information on volumes and scope is usually enough to quote. If you do send them, sign an NDA first.

How many providers should I invite?

Three is usually enough for a meaningful comparison without spending too much time on scoring.

Should a quote far below the others be a concern?

Re-check the scope: does it include group reporting, tax finalization and audit coordination, and how are additional fees charged?

Does the RFP have to be written in Vietnamese?

No. Many FDI companies send RFPs in English; it is worth checking each provider's ability to respond in English at this stage.
