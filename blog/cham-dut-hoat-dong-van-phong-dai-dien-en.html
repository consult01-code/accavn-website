Closing a Foreign Representative Office: Procedures and Tax

A checklist for closing a representative office: staff, PIT, tax code, bank account and the points that prolong the process.

A representative office (RO – văn phòng đại diện) does not carry out profit-making business, so many parent companies assume closing it will be simple. In reality, closing a representative office still requires dealing with staff, finalizing personal income tax, and closing the tax code and bank account in the right order. ACCA Consulting Vietnam summarizes the process and the mistakes that drag the file out for months.

1. How does closing a representative office differ from dissolving a company?

An RO is a dependent unit of a foreign trader, with no separate legal personality, and it may not conduct business directly. Its tax obligations are therefore mainly the personal income tax (PIT/TNCN) of its staff and certain other withholdings, with no corporate income tax finalization as for a company. However, the RO still has a tax code and may have a bank account, a seal, an office lease and employment contracts that need to be closed out.

2. Dealing with staff and social insurance

This is usually the most sensitive stage. Notify employees early and calculate in full their salary, untaken annual leave and allowances under their contracts and current regulations. Then close out each person's social insurance record.

As a reminder of the mandatory insurance rates for checking the final month: the enterprise/unit contributes 21.5% and the employee 10.5% of the salary subject to social insurance (BHXH). 3. PIT finalization and closing the tax code

As an income-paying organization, the RO must withhold, declare and finalize PIT for its staff up to the termination date. For foreign employees leaving Vietnam, remember to finalize before departure. See the general guidance in PIT finalization.

No PIT return is required for a month or quarter in which no PIT was withheld. However, when closing the tax code the tax authority reviews the entire history, so any period with tax withheld but not declared will have to be dealt with before closure.

5. Mistakes that prolong the process

  • Closing the bank account too early: penalties or late-payment interest then arise and there is no account to pay them from.
  • Filing the final-period return late: a late-filing penalty applies; for organizations 1–30 days late, VND 2–5 million (Decree 125/2020/ND-CP and its amending documents).
  • No one left in Vietnam: the RO's chief representative returns home and no one can sign or receive notices from the tax authority.
  • Lost documents: the income paid cannot be proven, requiring repeated explanations.
  • Foreign contractor tax overlooked: payments to foreign suppliers that were never declared.

FAQ

Does an RO have to submit financial statements when it closes?

An RO does not prepare financial statements like a company, but it must still keep books and records of operating fund receipts and payments for explanation purposes. Periodic activity reports required by regulations must still be completed.

What if the RO's chief representative is a foreigner who has already returned home?

The parent company can authorize another person in Vietnam to carry out the procedures. The power of attorney must be legalized as required by the receiving authority.

Should we convert the RO into a subsidiary instead of closing it?

If the parent company wants to do business in Vietnam, setting up a subsidiary is the common route. The RO can then be closed once the new company is operating smoothly.
