VAT Refunds for New FDI Investment Projects: Files and Pitfalls

How to compile input VAT during construction and machinery imports, and prepare the refund file so it is not delayed.

While building the factory and importing machinery, an FDI company spends heavily but has no revenue yet, so input VAT builds up into a significant sum. A VAT refund for an investment project lets you recover this cash, provided the file is clean from the very first document. ACCA Consulting Vietnam summarizes the preparation process and the mistakes that most often send a file into a pre-refund inspection.

1. When is a new investment project considered for a VAT refund?

In principle, a new investment project still in the investment phase and not yet operating may be considered for a refund if it has input value-added tax (VAT/GTGT) that has not been fully credited. The specific conditions (tax amount thresholds, excluded sectors, capital contribution conditions, licences for conditional business lines, etc.) change from period to period, so check the current regulations before preparing the file.

For FDI companies, the tax authority usually scrutinizes three points:

  • Capital actually contributed on time: capital must be transferred through the direct investment capital account (DICA) and contributed within 90 days of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC) issue date. Under-contribution or late contribution is a major weakness in the file.
  • Non-cash payment: invoices of VND 5 million or more (VAT inclusive) must be paid through a bank for the input VAT to be creditable.
  • Input VAT genuinely serves the project: invoices must show the company's correct name, address and tax code; goods and services must relate to the investment items.

2. Tracking input VAT during construction

A common mistake is waiting until you are ready to file before gathering documents. Open a separate project tracker from the first month, broken down by item: construction, imported machinery, locally purchased equipment, consulting services. Each line should reference the invoice number, payment voucher, contract and acceptance minutes.

A cumulative chart makes it easy for management to see how much money is "waiting" with the state budget and to time the filing to fit the cash flow plan.

4. Mistakes that send the file to a pre-refund inspection

  1. Capital not fully contributed or contributed late: using loans from the parent company instead of contributed capital without properly completing the foreign loan procedures.
  2. Invoices from the pre-incorporation stage: costs paid by the investor before the ERC was issued, but the invoices are not in the company's name.
  3. Offsetting or third-party payments without documentation: the parent company pays the contractor on the company's behalf without a tripartite agreement.
  4. Mixing project costs with operating costs: when the project already has some revenue but input VAT is not separated.
  5. Risky suppliers: the seller has ceased operating or abandoned its business address.
  6. Discrepancies between returns and the books: returns amended several times without explanation.
An incorrect refund claim may result in recovery of the amount refunded, together with late-payment interest of 0.03% per day and penalties under the regulations. Clean up the file before submitting it; do not file first and fix later.

FAQ

Can a project that already has some revenue still get an investment VAT refund?

It depends on whether the project has officially commenced operations and how input VAT is separated. This situation easily leads to disputes, so review it carefully with a specialist before filing.

If we do not claim a refund, is the input VAT lost?

No. The company can carry it forward to offset against output VAT once it has revenue. A refund simply recovers the cash sooner.

Do invoices issued before the ERC count?

Pre-incorporation costs require valid documents and a legal basis for transferring them to the company. Invoices not in the company's name are usually difficult to accept for crediting or refund.
