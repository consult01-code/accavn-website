Work Permit Exemptions in Vietnam: How to Check Correctly

How to check whether a foreign national is exempt from a work permit, and the confirmation procedure that still applies.

"He owns the company, so he's exempt from a work permit" is something many FDI companies hear. In practice, an exemption does not mean there is nothing to do. In most cases a written confirmation is still required, and tax and insurance obligations still follow. This guide on how to check correctly was compiled by the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. Three routes: work permit, confirmed exemption, or no procedure

The rules on foreign workers divide employees into several groups. To keep things practical, accounting and HR can reduce them to the three routes below. The exact names and list of cases must be taken from the decree currently in force, as this content has changed through successive amendments.

2. Common work permit exemptions in FDI companies

Owners, capital-contributing members and board members

An investor who is the owner or a capital-contributing member of a limited liability company, or the chairman or a board member of a joint stock company, may not be subject to a work permit if the value of their capital contribution reaches the statutory threshold. Check two points: whether the contribution is measured as registered capital or capital actually paid in, and whether the person also holds another executive title.

Intra-corporate transferees

Managers, executive directors, experts and technical workers seconded by a group to its commercial presence in Vietnam, within the service sectors covered by Vietnam's WTO commitments, may be exempt. Conditions usually include a minimum period of prior employment within the group and an assignment letter. You need to check whether the Vietnamese company's business lines are included in the schedule of commitments.

Short-term work

People entering Vietnam to offer services, resolve complex technical incidents, or work for a short total period within the year may be exempt, subject to the statutory time and frequency thresholds. Companies should keep a log of each person's entry and exit dates to provide evidence when needed.

4. Common misconceptions about work permit exemptions

  • "Exempt means nothing to do": many cases still require a written confirmation, and that confirmation has a validity period.
  • "Exempt means no insurance contributions": compulsory insurance coverage is assessed under its own conditions, not automatically based on work permit status.
  • "No work permit means no PIT withholding": income from work performed in Vietnam remains subject to personal income tax (PIT/TNCN) according to the person's residence status.
  • "One confirmation lasts forever": a change of title, capital ownership percentage or employer can all remove the grounds for exemption.
  • "A business visa is enough to work": a visa only permits entry for a stated purpose; it does not replace a work permit or a written confirmation.
Many articles online still cite outdated decrees on foreign workers. Always check the effective date of a regulation before relying on it to conclude that someone is exempt.

FAQ

Does the confirmation of not being subject to a work permit expire?

Yes. The confirmation usually has a validity period set by the rules and by the expected period of work. Once it expires, the company must apply again if the employee continues to work.

What if a foreign board member also serves as executive director?

Each title needs to be assessed. The exemption for board members may not cover an executive role, so check carefully and ask the licensing authority if anything is unclear.

If exempt from a work permit, is a temporary residence card still needed?

The temporary residence card is a separate immigration procedure. The confirmation of not being subject to a work permit is usually one of the documents used to apply for the appropriate card.
