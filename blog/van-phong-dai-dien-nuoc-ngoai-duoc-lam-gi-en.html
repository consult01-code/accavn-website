Representative Office in Vietnam: What It Can and Cannot Do

The activity limits of a foreign representative office, its PIT and insurance obligations, and the warning signs of permanent establishment risk.

Many foreign companies open a representative office in Vietnam because the procedure is simple and running costs are low. The risk is that the line between “promotion” and “trading” is thin: if the chief representative signs a few contracts on behalf of the parent company, the tax picture changes. This article sets out what a representative office (RO) may do, what it may not do, and its accounting obligations, from the perspective of the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. What is a foreign representative office?

A representative office (RO – văn phòng đại diện, VPĐD) is a dependent unit of a foreign trader. An RO is not a Vietnamese legal entity, has no charter capital and may not directly carry out profit-generating business. All rights and obligations arising from the RO's activities belong to the parent company abroad.

An RO therefore suits the exploratory stage: researching the market, meeting partners and supporting the parent company's existing customers. Once there are regular orders in Vietnam, investors should consider a separate legal entity; see comparing forms of investment in Vietnam.

4. Tax, insurance and reporting obligations of an RO

Because it does not trade, an RO usually has no value-added tax (VAT/GTGT) or corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) arising from sales. However, an RO is still an income-paying organization, so it has the following main obligations:

  • Personal income tax (PIT/TNCN): withhold tax from employees' salaries and file monthly or quarterly; no PIT return is required for a period with no tax withheld. The income payer's annual PIT finalization is due on the last day of the third month after year-end (31 March for a calendar year).
  • Family deductions from the 2026 tax period: VND 15.5 million/month for the taxpayer (≈ USD 608, approximate, at a reference rate of VND 25,500/USD) and VND 6.2 million/month for each dependant.
  • Compulsory insurance for Vietnamese employees: the RO contributes 21.5% and the employee 10.5% of the salary subject to social insurance.
  • Periodic activity reports submitted to the licensing authority under current regulations.
  • Receipts and payments records: track funds transferred by the parent company and all expenses, and keep source documents used directly for bookkeeping for at least 10 years.

Illustrative payroll cost for one employee

Illustrative example: an RO pays a Vietnamese employee a salary subject to social insurance of VND 15 million/month. The RO's insurance contribution is 15,000,000 × 21.5% = VND 3,225,000; the amount deducted from the employee's salary is 15,000,000 × 10.5% = VND 1,575,000. The RO's total cost for this employee is VND 18,225,000/month, before other benefits.

5. When should you move from an RO to a legal entity?

An RO has few transactions, but a single mistake in PIT filing or insurance is penalized just as it would be for a company. FAQ

Can a foreign representative office issue invoices?

No. An RO may not conduct profit-generating business, so it does not issue invoices for goods or services. Contracts and invoices must be issued by the parent company or by a legal entity licensed to trade.

Does an RO have to prepare financial statements like a company?

An RO does not prepare financial statements like a trading company, but it must track receipts and payments, file PIT returns and submit periodic activity reports as required. The specific bookkeeping approach should be agreed with the parent company.

Does a foreign chief representative pay PIT in Vietnam?

Possibly, depending on tax residency status and who pays the income. The number of days present in Vietnam and any relevant tax treaties should be established before filing.
