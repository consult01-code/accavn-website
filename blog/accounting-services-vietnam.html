1. Why foreign companies need local accounting support

A company with foreign capital in Vietnam is a Vietnamese legal entity. It keeps its books under Vietnamese rules, files tax returns with the Vietnamese tax authority and issues electronic invoices through the national system. Head-office reporting under IFRS or US GAAP does not replace any of this.

In practice, foreign directors face three gaps. The first is language: most returns, invoices and official letters are in Vietnamese. The second is timing: deadlines are monthly, quarterly and annual, and late filing triggers administrative fines. The third is structure: foreign-invested companies have extra obligations, such as a dedicated capital account and an audited annual financial statement.

Professional accounting services in Vietnam close these gaps. A good provider records transactions, prepares returns, reconciles bank accounts and explains the results to you in English, so you can make decisions without reading Vietnamese tax forms yourself.

2. What a foreign-invested company must file in 2026

The core obligations below come from the Law on Tax Administration 38/2019/QH14 and Decree 126/2020/ND-CP. A new Law on Tax Administration (2025) took effect on 1 July 2026, so the details should always be checked against the latest guidance.

Newly established companies file VAT quarterly. After that, companies with total revenue from goods and services of 50 billion VND or less in the previous year may file quarterly; above that level, filing is monthly. The four quarterly CIT payments together must not be lower than 80% of the CIT payable in the annual finalization.

Tax rates worth knowing

  • CIT: the standard rate is 20%. Under the 2025 CIT Law (67/2025/QH15), companies with annual revenue up to 3 billion VND pay 15%, and above 3 up to 50 billion VND pay 17%.
  • VAT: rates are 0%, 5% and 10%. A 2% reduction (10% down to 8%) for certain goods and services has been extended to 31 December 2026, with excluded groups.
  • Business license fee: abolished from 1 January 2026 (Resolution 198/2025/QH15).
  • Social insurance: the employer pays 21.5% and the employee 10.5% of the insured salary.

3. FDI-specific rules that local companies do not face

Foreign investors usually obtain an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC) first and then an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC). The IRC is processed within 15 days of a complete, valid dossier (where investment policy approval is not required), and the ERC within 3 working days. See our Vietnamese guide on setting up a foreign-owned company for the full procedure.

  • Direct investment capital account (DICA): charter capital must flow through this dedicated account.
  • Capital contribution deadline: capital must be contributed within 90 days of the ERC issuance date.
  • Statutory audit: annual financial statements must be audited by an independent audit firm.
  • E-invoices: mandatory for all enterprises (Decree 123/2020, amended by Decree 70/2025).
  • Records: accounting documents used for bookkeeping and financial statements must be kept for at least 10 years (Accounting Law 2015).
Input VAT on purchases of 5 million VND or more (VAT included) is only creditable when paid by non-cash means, under the 2024 VAT Law effective 1 July 2025. Cash payments to suppliers are one of the most common reasons foreign-invested companies lose VAT credits.

FAQ

Can a foreign director handle Vietnamese tax filings without a local accountant?

Legally the company is responsible for its filings, not the provider. In practice, returns, e-invoices and tax notices are in Vietnamese and deadlines are frequent, so most foreign-owned companies use a local accountant or an outsourced service.

Do small foreign-invested companies still need an annual audit?

Yes. Foreign-invested companies must have their annual financial statements audited by an independent audit firm, regardless of size. Your accountant prepares the statements and supports the auditor.
