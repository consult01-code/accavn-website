Expat Salary Paid from Abroad: How to Declare Tax in Vietnam

Offshore and split payroll: who files the tax, whether recharge costs are deductible, and the cash flow points to control.

An expat who works in Vietnam but receives part or all of their salary paid from abroad is a very common situation in FDI companies. Money that does not pass through the Vietnamese company's bank account still has to be declared. This article helps accountants and expats determine who files, what to file and which costs are deductible, following the accounting services approach of ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. Offshore payroll vs. split payroll

Offshore payroll means the expat's entire salary is paid by the parent company or another group company abroad. Split payroll means the salary is divided in two: one part is paid in Vietnam through the FDI entity and the rest is paid abroad, usually into an account in the expat's home country.

In principle, if the expat is a tax resident of Vietnam, personal income tax (PIT/TNCN) on salaries and wages is calculated on total income, wherever it is paid. If the expat is non-resident, the taxable scope is income arising in Vietnam. To determine residence, see PIT residence for foreign experts.

2. Who declares the salary paid from abroad

For individuals finalizing their own PIT, the deadline is the last day of the 4th month after the end of the year (30 April for a calendar year). In practice, many companies help expats declare their foreign salary through a service provider, but responsibility for the figures should still be clearly agreed in writing.

3. Recharge costs: when are they deductible?

When the parent company pays the salary and then recharges it to the Vietnamese entity, the key question is whether this payment is a deductible expense for corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN). Experience shows that the tax authority looks at the substance: who the expat works for, who directs the work, and whether the documents reflect that substance.

If the recharge includes a mark-up or is labelled a "service fee", it may give rise to foreign contractor tax (FCT) and fall within the scope of related-party transactions. Do not book it together with salary costs.

4. Cash flow map and control points

The most important control is that salary figures in three places must match: the parent company's payroll, the expat's tax returns and the salary costs in the Vietnamese company's books. Discrepancies between these three sources are a red flag that the tax authority often raises during finalization audits.

5. Common risks

  • The expat does not know they must self-declare the foreign salary, discovers it only at finalization and has to pay additional tax plus late payment interest of 0.03% per day.
  • Recharges without an agreement, supported only by a debit note, leading to the cost being disallowed for CIT.
  • Omitting benefits paid by the parent company (housing, school fees, foreign insurance) from taxable income. See taxable expat benefits.
  • Not considering the tax treaty when the expat has already paid tax in another country on the same salary.

FAQ

Is tax withheld in Vietnam on salary paid from abroad?

The Vietnamese company does not pay this portion directly, so it usually does not withhold on it. A resident expat must file directly with the tax authority for the prescribed periods, unless there is a valid arrangement for filing on their behalf.

Can the Vietnamese company declare the foreign salary on the expat's behalf?

In some cases, an organization in Vietnam may file on the expat's behalf under current guidance. The company should have a written agreement and check the specific conditions before doing so.

Are salary recharge costs subject to foreign contractor tax?

A reimbursement equal to the salary cost is usually treated differently from a service fee. If a mark-up or management fee is added, foreign contractor tax obligations need to be analysed separately.

How is the foreign salary handled if the expat leaves mid-year?

The expat must finalize tax before leaving Vietnam as required, combining income from inside and outside Vietnam. See also the article on expat PIT in the first and final years.
