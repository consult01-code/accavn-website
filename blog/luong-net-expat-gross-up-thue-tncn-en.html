Net Salary for Expats in Vietnam: Grossing Up for PIT

How to convert an expat's net salary to gross step by step, with a housing example and an Excel template to avoid underpaying tax.

Contracts for foreign experts often state a net salary: the company commits to paying an after-tax amount and bears all personal income tax (PIT/TNCN). In that case the accountant must gross up the net salary, i.e. convert it back to pre-tax income, because the tax the company pays on the employee's behalf is itself taxable income. This article explains the method step by step, with an example that includes a housing allowance. It draws on the payroll accounting experience of ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. Why gross up a net salary?

If the company pays PIT on the employee's behalf, that tax is a benefit to the employee. Taxable income must therefore include the tax paid on their behalf. Calculating tax directly on the net salary will always understate it, because it ignores the "tax on the tax".

The consequence of underpayment: the company withholds and pays too little PIT, and is assessed for the shortfall plus late payment interest of 0.03% per day. In addition, the salary expense recorded does not match the PIT returns, which causes difficulties at the corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) finalization.

2. Step-by-step method for converting net to gross

Steps 3 and 4 require the progressive tax schedule currently in force. Because the schedule may change under amended legislation, the example below uses a hypothetical single-rate schedule of 30% purely to illustrate the conversion logic. Do not use these figures to calculate actual tax.

3. Gross-up example with a housing allowance

Expert M (illustrative example) is a resident individual whose contract states: net salary of VND 80 million/month (≈ USD 3,140, approximate at VND 25,500/USD), plus rent of VND 20 million/month paid by the company directly to the landlord, with the company bearing all tax. No dependants. All rent is assumed to be taxable income (in practice there is a cap under current regulations), and insurance is not considered.

The difference is about VND 10.86 million per month, or more than VND 130 million per year, for just one expert in this example. Under the actual progressive schedule the difference depends on the bracket, but the direction is always an underpayment.

4. Excel template for net-to-gross conversion

There are two ways to build the template. The first uses a bracket conversion table: each tax bracket has a formula converting net income to gross assessable income, with IFS or LOOKUP used to select the bracket. The second uses Goal Seek: enter a trial gross, calculate tax using the progressive schedule, then let Excel find the gross at which gross − tax = net.

  • Keep a separate parameters sheet: tax schedule, deduction levels, insurance rates. When the law changes, you update only one place.
  • One row per expat, with columns for net cash, items paid on their behalf, deductions, gross, tax and back-check.
  • Add a warning flag column when the back-check row is not 0.
  • State the exchange rate if the net contract is in USD, and the date the rate applies.
  • Lock formulas and leave only input cells editable to prevent accidental changes.

5. Common errors when the company bears the tax

At finalization, you must also gross up again on full-year income. If the finalized tax exceeds the total withheld, the additional amount paid by the company is again income and needs to be converted further. For the general finalization process, see PIT finalization.

FAQ

Is PIT paid by the company on the employee's behalf a deductible expense?

Tax paid on the employee's behalf is usually treated as part of salary if it is clearly stated in the employment contract and salary policy and has been grossed up correctly. Check the conditions for deductible expenses under current regulations.

Which exchange rate is used to gross up a USD net salary?

Convert to VND at the exchange rate used for tax filing at the time of payment, then gross up. Set out the exchange rate policy in the salary policy so that it is applied consistently.

Is gross-up needed for a non-resident expat?

Yes, if the company commits to a net salary. The calculation is simpler because the flat statutory rate applies and there are no family deductions.

Should AI tools be used to calculate gross-up?

An AI assistant can be used to check formulas, but expat salary and passport data should not be uploaded to public AI tools. The final result still needs to be checked against the current tax schedule.
