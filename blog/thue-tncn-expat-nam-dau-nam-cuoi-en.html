Expat PIT in Vietnam: First and Final Year, a Worked Case

A hypothetical expert arriving in September 2026 and leaving Vietnam in May 2028: tax periods, deductions and finalization.

The first and final years are the two periods where expat personal income tax is most often miscalculated: the expert is not in Vietnam for the full year, the tax period may not match the calendar year, and finalization must be completed before they leave the country. This article walks through a hypothetical case with calculation tables. It was prepared by the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam for payroll accountants in FDI companies.

1. Hypothetical case: expert K works 21 months in Vietnam

Expert K (illustrative example) is seconded by the group to serve as plant director in Ho Chi Minh City. Key facts:

In calendar year 2026, K is present for only 122 days, short of 183 days. However, K holds a temporary residence card, and counting 12 consecutive months from 01/09/2026, K is present for more than 183 days. K is therefore a resident individual. How to determine residence is explained in detail in PIT residence for foreign nationals.

2. Expat PIT in the first year: a tax period that differs from the calendar year

Under current guidance, if an individual is present for fewer than 183 days in the first calendar year but reaches 183 days within 12 consecutive months from the first day of presence, the first tax period is the 12 consecutive months starting from that first day. From the second year onward, the tax period follows the calendar year. The months that overlap between the first period and the second year are handled according to the guidance so that income is not taxed twice.

At finalization, if the full annual personal deduction is allowed, K may have overpaid tax. Conversely, if K receives an additional bonus or a termination payment, more tax may be payable.

4. Expat PIT finalization before leaving Vietnam

A foreign national whose employment in Vietnam ends must finalize their tax before leaving the country. In practice, the individual usually authorizes the company to do this on their behalf. The company should fix the schedule as soon as the termination decision is made, because once the expert has left it is very hard to obtain signatures and documents.

5. Common mistakes with expat PIT in the first and final years

  • Treating a first-year expat as non-resident simply because they have not reached 183 days in the calendar year.
  • Omitting salary and bonuses paid abroad by the group while K is a resident individual. See expat salary paid from abroad.
  • Not recalculating tax when the expat receives a large bonus just before leaving Vietnam.
  • Letting the expat leave before finalization, leaving signatures and documents missing.
  • Applying the previous year's tax schedule and deduction levels to tax periods from 2026.

FAQ

Is an expat who spends fewer than 183 days in Vietnam in the first year definitely non-resident?

Not necessarily. You also need to consider the 12 consecutive months from the first day of presence and the habitual residence criteria, such as a temporary residence card or a house lease.

Can the company finalize tax on behalf of an expat who has left the job?

The company may accept an authorization to finalize, subject to the statutory conditions. If the expat has income from other sources or from abroad, they usually have to finalize themselves or declare that income additionally.

How is overpaid tax from the final-year finalization handled?

The individual can request a refund to a registered bank account. Prepare the receiving account and the authorization letter before the expat leaves Vietnam.
