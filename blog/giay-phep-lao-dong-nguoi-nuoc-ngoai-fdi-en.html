Work Permits for Foreigners in Vietnam: What FDI Companies Need

An overview of the work permit and temporary residence card process for foreign experts, and the accounting obligations that come with it.

Almost every FDI company has a foreign director or expert. Before that person starts work, the company must obtain a work permit for the foreign employee (WP – giấy phép lao động, GPLĐ) or confirm that the person is exempt. This article summarizes the steps, the documents, how to plan the timeline and how the permit links to payroll, insurance and personal income tax. The content was compiled by the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam from hands-on work with FDI clients.

1. What is a work permit for a foreigner?

A work permit is issued by the provincial labor authority and allows a foreign national to work for a specific employer, in a specific position and for a specific period. The permit is tied to the company: moving to another company or changing position usually means going through the procedure again.

The rules on foreign workers have changed several times in recent years, including which authority receives applications after the government's administrative restructuring. Before filing, you should therefore re-check the decree currently in force, the forms and the local place of submission. Without a valid work permit, both the employee and the company can be penalized, and the employee may be deported.

2. Steps to obtain a work permit for a foreign employee

If the employee is exempt, the company may still need to obtain a confirmation that the person is not subject to a work permit. How to check each case is covered in foreigners who are not subject to a work permit.

3. Personal documents and planning the timeline

The most time-consuming part is usually not the licensing authority but the foreign documents. Many must be consular-legalized (unless exempt under a treaty) and translated and certified into Vietnamese. Some documents have a short validity period, so they need to be sequenced so that none expires before filing.

4. How does the work permit relate to the contract, insurance and PIT?

For the accounting team, the work permit is more than an HR document. It directly affects how salaries are recorded and what tax obligations arise:

  • Employment contract: the contract term must not exceed the work permit term. The contract is the basis for salary costs to be deductible for corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN).
  • Compulsory insurance: foreign employees with a work permit and an employment contract may be subject to compulsory social insurance (BHXH) and health insurance (BHYT) under current conditions. See also social insurance for foreign employees.
  • Personal income tax (PIT/TNCN): the company withholds tax monthly; whether the employee is tax resident or non-resident determines how tax is calculated and whether family deductions apply.
  • Temporary residence card: residence documents are one of the bases for determining a habitual place of residence for PIT purposes.

FAQ

Does a director who is also the foreign investor need a work permit?

It depends. An owner, capital-contributing member or board member who meets the capital contribution threshold under current regulations may not be subject to a work permit, but you need to check the confirmation procedure.

Can an expert start work before the work permit is issued?

It is not advisable. Working without a work permit or a valid confirmation can lead to penalties. The company should use a visa that matches the purpose of the visit and wait until all documents are in place before assigning formal work.
