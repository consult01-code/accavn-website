Setting Up a Foreign-Owned Company in HCMC: IRC & ERC Steps

The IRC – ERC process, the DICA capital account, the capital contribution deadline and the accounting and tax tasks right after licensing.

Setting up a foreign-owned company in Ho Chi Minh City usually involves two certificates: first the IRC, then the ERC. The difficult part is not only the licensing paperwork but what comes after: the capital account, contributing capital on time, and accounting and tax from the very first month. This article summarizes the process from the perspective of the ACCA Consulting Vietnam team, an accounting firm that works alongside many foreign investors.

1. What are the IRC and ERC, and why do you need both?

When a foreign investor establishes a business entity in Vietnam, it normally first needs an Investment Registration Certificate (IRC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký đầu tư). The IRC records the project: the investor, objectives, scale, capital, location and timeline. Only after obtaining the IRC does the company apply for an Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký doanh nghiệp), which formally creates the legal entity with its enterprise code and tax code.

Put simply, the IRC answers the question "may this investment project go ahead?", while the ERC answers "which company will carry out the project?". The two certificates are closely linked. Information on capital, business lines and investors must be consistent; any discrepancy will require amendments and cost extra time.

3. The DICA account and the 90-day capital contribution deadline

A foreign investor's capital contribution must be transferred through a direct investment capital account (DICA) opened at a licensed bank. Do not send capital to an ordinary current account or contribute in cash: it will be difficult to prove the capital was validly contributed during an audit, when repatriating profits, or when transferring capital later.

  • Deadline for full contribution: 90 days from the ERC issue date.
  • State clearly in the transfer description that it is a charter capital contribution, matching the investor's name on the IRC/ERC.
  • Keep the DICA statements, bank documents and capital contribution minutes for the audit.
  • If the capital is not fully contributed on time, the company must carry out adjustment procedures under current regulations; seek advice early.

4. Accounting and tax tasks immediately after the ERC

Many investors think the job is done once they have the ERC. In reality, accounting and tax obligations start immediately. Under the regulations, newly established companies file value-added tax (VAT/GTGT) returns quarterly, due on the last day of the first month of the following quarter. From 1 January 2026, the business license fee (lệ phí môn bài) has been abolished under Resolution 198/2025/QH15, so new companies no longer pay it.

Each task is explained in detail in what to do after setting up a company. For FDI companies, note also that annual financial statements must be audited by an independent audit firm, so the books need to be clean from the first year.

6. Common mistakes when setting up a foreign-owned company

  1. Capital and business line details on the IRC and ERC don't match, requiring repeated amendments.
  2. Transferring capital into a current account instead of the DICA.
  3. Capital still not fully contributed after 90 days.
  4. Spending before the ERC is issued without valid documents in the company's name.
  5. Paying invoices of VND 5 million or more in cash, losing the right to deduct input VAT.
  6. Leaving the books empty for the first few months and rebuilding them only when the audit begins.

FAQ

How long does it take to set up a foreign-owned company?

By law, the IRC is processed within 15 days of receiving a complete, valid application (where investment policy approval is not required), and the ERC within 3 working days. In practice it usually takes longer because of preparation, translation and legalization of foreign documents.

Is opening a DICA mandatory?

Yes. A foreign investor's capital contribution must be transferred through a direct investment capital account. It is key evidence during audits and when repatriating profits.

Does a small foreign-owned company need an audit?

Yes. FDI companies must have their annual financial statements audited by an independent audit firm, regardless of size.
