Bilingual Vietnamese–English Accounting Records: Rules and Setup

Language requirements for vouchers and books, and how to organize bilingual Vietnamese–English accounting records in software for FDI companies.

The foreign director wants to read the general ledger, while the tax authority requires Vietnamese. Bilingual accounting records let a single set of data serve both. This article explains the language requirements under Vietnamese accounting law, how to handle documents in foreign languages and how to organize everything in your software, based on the experience of ACCA Consulting Vietnam in providing accounting services to FDI companies.

1. What does the law require on the accounting language?

The Law on Accounting (Luật Kế toán) states that the script used in accounting is Vietnamese. When a company needs to use a foreign language on vouchers, accounting books and financial statements, it must use both Vietnamese and the foreign language at the same time. In other words, bilingual records are allowed, but Vietnamese cannot be dropped.

For documents originating in a foreign language (invoices from overseas suppliers, contracts with the parent company, English-language bank statements), current regulations require the main content to be translated into Vietnamese when used for bookkeeping. The exact scope of translation for each document type should be checked case by case; for long contracts, companies usually translate the clauses on value, payment and obligations.

2. How to organize bilingual accounting records in your software

Bilingual records do not mean keeping two sets of books. The effective approach is one dataset, two display layers: every master list has two name fields, and every report can be printed in Vietnamese, English or side by side. When choosing software, check the following points.

A standard description library

The most time-consuming part is writing the description for each voucher. Build a library of a few dozen standard descriptions for recurring transactions, for example: "Thanh toán tiền thuê văn phòng tháng 09/2026 / Office rental payment for 09/2026". The accountant only changes the month, invoice number and counterparty. The data stays consistent and is easy to filter during an audit.

5. Common mistakes with bilingual records

  • Payment vouchers or goods received notes only in English: the documents lack the Vietnamese required by the Law on Accounting.
  • Inconsistent translations of account names from month to month, complicating group reporting.
  • Contracts with the parent only in English, with no translation of the payment clauses when the tax authority inspects.
  • Using public machine translation tools for contracts and payroll data: a risk of data leakage. Use internal tools or tools with a confidentiality commitment.

FAQ

Can an FDI company keep its books entirely in English?

No. Vietnamese is mandatory; English may only be used alongside Vietnamese on vouchers, books and reports.

Do invoices from foreign suppliers need to be translated?

When used for bookkeeping, their main content must be translated into Vietnamese and the translation kept with the original. The scope of translation should be checked for each document type.

Do audited financial statements need to be bilingual?

The version filed with State authorities must be in Vietnamese. Many FDI companies ask their auditor to issue a bilingual version for the group.

Do bilingual records cost much more?

Mostly it is the initial setup effort. Once the master lists and description library are in place, the monthly workload increases only marginally.
