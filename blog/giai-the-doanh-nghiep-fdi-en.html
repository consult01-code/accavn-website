Dissolving an FDI Company: From Project Termination to Tax Code

A stage-by-stage roadmap for dissolving a foreign-owned company, with time estimates and the points that tend to prolong the process.

Dissolving an FDI company is more complex than dissolving a domestic company because three "files" must be handled at once: the investment project (IRC), the enterprise registration (ERC) and tax obligations, plus the outflow of capital through the DICA. This article by ACCA Consulting Vietnam sets out the tasks in the right order so investors can plan the timeline and avoid detours.

1. Before deciding to dissolve an FDI company

Dissolution is only possible when the company can pay all its debts and other property obligations in full. Before making the decision, management should therefore have a clear financial picture:

  • A list of payables: suppliers, the parent company, employees, the tax authority, social insurance.
  • A list of assets that can be liquidated: machinery, inventory, deposits, receivables.
  • Contracts still in force: office leases, land leases, employment contracts, service contracts.
  • The state of the books: years already audited, years not yet finalized, risk of back-tax assessments.

If the books still have many open items, review your accounting records first. A small unresolved issue can delay the closure of the tax code by several months.

4. Tax obligations when dissolving an FDI company

If a missed filing period is discovered, file a supplementary return as soon as possible. Late-payment interest is charged at 0.03% per day on the tax paid late, and penalties for late filing of tax returns can range from VND 2–5 million (1–30 days late) up to VND 15–25 million for organizations (under Decree 125/2020/ND-CP and its amending documents).

5. Repatriating remaining capital and closing the DICA

Once all debts and tax obligations have been paid, the remaining funds belong to the foreign investor. These funds must flow through the direct investment capital account (DICA). Banks usually require documents proving the source of funds and that obligations have been fulfilled.

FAQ

Can we suspend operations instead of dissolving?

Yes, if the investor is not yet sure about exiting completely. Suspension still carries some reporting obligations, but it preserves the legal entity and the project so you can resume later.

Can we dissolve while still owing money to the parent company?

The debt to the parent company must also be dealt with: repaid, converted into capital through the proper procedure, or waived by the parent in writing. Each option has different tax consequences, which should be calculated in advance.

Is an audit of the financial statements mandatory in the year of dissolution?

FDI companies must have their annual financial statements audited. For the dissolution period, discuss the documentation requirements with the tax authority and the bank in advance to avoid having to redo the work.
