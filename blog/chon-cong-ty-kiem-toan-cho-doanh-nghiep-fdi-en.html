Choosing an Auditor for an FDI Company: Big Four or Mid-Tier?

A weighted scorecard to help FDI companies choose the right audit firm instead of comparing only fees or brand names.

Every year, an FDI company must engage an independent audit firm for its annual financial statements. The question is not only what it costs but which firm can meet the group's requirements, understands the industry and keeps to the timetable. This article sets out a comparison framework and a scorecard so you can choose an audit firm on a sound basis. It is written from the company accountant's perspective by the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. Start from the group's requirements, not the price list

Before requesting quotes, ask the parent company three questions: does the group appoint a global audit firm; must the subsidiary submit a reporting package for the group audit; and do banks or partners require a specific audit brand? If the answer to all three is "yes", the choice has more or less been made.

If the group lets the subsidiary choose, the company has more options. The minimum mandatory condition: the audit firm and its practising auditors must be eligible to provide audit services under current regulations. Ask the firm to provide evidence of this in its proposal.

2. Big Four vs. mid-tier and small audit firms

There is no choice that is right for every company. A small trading company whose group does not require a specific firm is often well served by a mid-tier firm with FDI experience. A factory belonging to a listed group usually needs a large international network to align with the group audit process.

FAQ

Must an FDI company use a Big Four firm?

No. The regulations only require the annual financial statements to be audited by an eligible independent audit firm; choosing a Big Four firm usually stems from group or partner requirements.

Should we choose the firm with the lowest fee?

Only if that firm also meets the other important criteria. Use the scorecard to compare the overall picture, including additional fees and the risk of missing deadlines.

Can our accounting service provider also audit the company?

It is not advisable and is generally not permitted, because it affects independence. Accounting and audit should be performed by two separate firms.
