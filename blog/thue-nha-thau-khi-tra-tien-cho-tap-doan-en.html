Every time an FDI company remits management fees, software fees or interest to its parent, the accountant must answer the question of foreign contractor tax (FCT – thuế nhà thầu nước ngoài) on payments to the parent company: must tax be withheld, and how? This article provides a reference table for each type of payment to the parent, explains how to check the tax treaty and lists the records to keep. It is the first area ACCA Consulting Vietnam usually reviews when taking on accounting for an FDI company.

1. Foreign contractor tax on payments to the parent: getting it right

When a foreign organization earns income arising in Vietnam under a contract with a Vietnamese company, the Vietnamese party is usually responsible for withholding and paying the tax on behalf of the foreign party. A parent company abroad is also a "contractor" in this sense. FCT generally has two components: a value-added tax (VAT/GTGT) component and a corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) component, depending on the nature of each payment.

FCT is a separate obligation and does not replace the deductibility conditions for management fees and royalties. A payment on which FCT has been paid in full can still be disallowed as a deductible expense if the actual benefit cannot be proven.

3. How to check the tax treaty with the parent company's country

Vietnam has signed double tax agreements with many countries. A treaty may exempt or cap the CIT on certain payments, such as business profits from services not provided through a permanent establishment, interest and royalties. Treaties generally do not apply to the VAT component.

Do not apply a treaty exemption on your own initiative without a certificate of residence and the notification file. This is a common mistake that leads to back-assessed FCT plus late-payment interest of 0.03% per day.

5. Filing timing and gross vs. net contracts

FCT is usually declared each time a payment is made. FDI companies should keep a payment schedule for the group, linked to the tax filing calendar, so nothing is missed.

Read the contract carefully: if the price paid to the parent excludes FCT (the Vietnamese party bears the tax on its behalf), the amount must be grossed up to determine taxable revenue. Whether the tax paid on the parent's behalf is a deductible expense must then also be considered under current regulations.

FAQ

The parent company has already paid tax in its own country. Does Vietnam still collect FCT?

There may still be an obligation in Vietnam because the income arises in Vietnam. Tax treaties help avoid double taxation; the parent can usually credit the tax paid in Vietnam under its own country's law.

Management fees are charged in USD. Which exchange rate applies?

Convert at the exchange rate prescribed for tax filing at the time the payment arises. Agree on one principle and apply it consistently to every filing.

What if we forgot to declare FCT in previous years?

Review all past payments, file supplementary returns and pay late-payment interest before the tax authority finds the issue. Correcting voluntarily and early usually reduces the risk of penalties significantly.
