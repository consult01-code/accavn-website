Export Processing Enterprises (EPE) in Vietnam: Accounting & Tax

Accounting and tax specifics of export processing enterprises: customs, domestic trade, VAT and goods finalization; compared with regular FDI companies.

An export processing enterprise (EPE – doanh nghiệp chế xuất) is treated like a non-tariff zone, so its bookkeeping, tax filing and inventory management differ substantially from those of a regular FDI manufacturer. Accountants used to domestic companies often struggle with purchases from and sales to the domestic market and with goods finalization reports to customs. This article summarizes the specific points the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam always checks when taking on an EPE.

1. What is an export processing enterprise and why is it different?

An export processing enterprise is a company that specializes in producing goods for export or providing services for export production, operating in an export processing zone, industrial park or economic zone. Under current regulations, an EPE must meet customs inspection and supervision requirements (a separating fence, camera system, inventory management software, etc.) and is subject to the rules applicable to non-tariff zones.

As a result, transactions between an EPE and the domestic market are treated as import and export transactions. Every shipment entering or leaving the EPE is tied to customs procedures, and the accounting books must reconcile with customs data.

3. VAT on purchases from and sales to the domestic market

When an EPE buys materials from a domestic supplier, the seller may issue an invoice at 0% VAT if the conditions on contracts, payment and customs procedures are met. If the seller still issues an invoice at 10% or 8%, the EPE generally cannot deduct that VAT as a domestic company would, which increases costs. Some services consumed inside the EPE (for example catering or internal transport) may still be subject to the standard rate, depending on the regulations.

When selling into the domestic market, an EPE needs to check the invoicing rules and the buyer's customs procedures. Recording domestic sales as ordinary export revenue is a very common mistake.

4. Goods finalization report to customs

An EPE must submit to customs a finalization report on imports, exports and inventory of raw materials, supplies and finished goods for each financial year, by the deadline set in current customs regulations. This is where accounting data and customs data are compared directly.

A practical tip: build a monthly reconciliation sheet with three columns for each material code: balance per the accounting books, balance per the warehouse software, and balance per customs data. Done monthly, this leaves only a few differences to resolve at year-end, instead of tracing a whole year of documents just before the finalization deadline.

5. Common accounting and tax mistakes at EPEs

  • Incorrectly deducting input VAT on 10% invoices for domestic purchases, later disallowed during an inspection.
  • Book inventory not matching customs inventory because scrap, samples and temporarily exported goods were not recorded.
  • Disposing of assets or scrap into the domestic market without completing customs procedures.
  • Missing CIT incentives or applying the conditions incorrectly; see CIT incentive conditions.

FAQ

Can an export processing enterprise sell into the domestic market?

Yes, but it must follow the import–export rules and customs procedures, and the domestic buyer pays the import-stage taxes as prescribed. The conditions in the Investment Registration Certificate (IRC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký đầu tư) should also be checked.

Does an EPE have to file VAT returns?

It depends on actual transactions and current guidance. The company should confirm with its managing tax office right after incorporation to set up the correct filing period.

How should an EPE handle a domestic purchase invoice showing 10% VAT?

Discuss with the seller whether the conditions for the 0% rate are met. If they are not, the VAT is usually considered as a cost rather than deducted; each case should be checked.
