Management Fees and Royalties to the Parent: Deductibility Rules

An evidence checklist so management fees and royalties paid to the parent company are accepted as deductible expenses, with an example of the cost if they are disallowed.

At year-end, many FDI companies receive a sizeable debit note for management fees paid to the parent company or for royalties. The charge makes sense from the group's point of view, but at tax finalization the Vietnamese company must prove that the service was real and delivered a benefit. This article provides an evidence checklist and the warning signs that make the expense likely to be disallowed, drawing on the accounting experience of ACCA Consulting Vietnam.

1. Why are management fees paid to the parent so often scrutinized?

Management fees, technical support fees and brand royalties are related party transactions that usually involve no tangible goods and are calculated using the group's allocation formula. The tax authority will ask three questions: was the service real, did the Vietnamese company benefit, and is the price consistent with the arm's length principle? If evidence is missing for any of these, the expense may be disallowed when calculating taxable income.

3. Warning signs that an expense is likely to be disallowed

  • Shareholder activities: costs incurred by the parent to manage its own investment (group board meetings, preparing consolidated financial statements) are allocated down to the subsidiary.
  • Duplicated services: the Vietnamese company already has its own HR and accounting departments but still pays for similar services.
  • No output: there is only a contract and a debit note, with no report or service deliverable.
  • Unclear allocation: rounded amounts, or large year-on-year changes that cannot be explained.
  • Persistent losses: the company keeps paying high fees to the parent, increasing its losses.
  • Brand royalties on contract manufacturing: the company only manufactures for the group yet still pays a fee to use the brand.

4. Example: the cost when VND 1 billion of management fees is disallowed

Assume an FDI company pays a management fee of VND 1 billion per year (approximately USD 39,000 at a reference rate of VND 25,500/USD), which is fully disallowed for lack of evidence in a tax audit one year (365 days) later. The CIT rate is 20% and late-payment interest is 0.03% per day on the tax underpaid, excluding any penalties.

5. Managing fees paid to the parent throughout the year

Evidence should be collected while the service is being delivered; trying to find it three years later, when the tax auditors arrive, is almost impossible. These fees must also be reflected correctly in the related party transaction appendix and the transfer pricing documentation.

FAQ

Are a contract and an invoice enough for the management fee to be deductible?

No. For related party transactions, the company also needs evidence that the service was real, delivered a benefit and was priced in line with the arm's length principle.

Is foreign contractor tax payable on fees paid to the parent company?

In many cases, yes, depending on the type of service and the double tax agreement between the two countries. Check the current regulations before paying.

Should we stop paying management fees while the company is making losses?

That is a business decision for the group. However, paying high fees while making persistent losses is a sign likely to attract scrutiny, so the evidence of benefit needs to be all the stronger.
