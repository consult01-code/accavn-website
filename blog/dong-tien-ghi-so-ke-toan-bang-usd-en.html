Using USD as the Accounting Currency in Vietnam: Conditions & Steps

Conditions for choosing USD as the accounting currency, the required procedures, translating financial statements into VND and the pros and cons.

Many FDI companies sell, buy materials and report to their parent in USD, so they would like to use a foreign currency as their accounting currency instead of VND. Vietnamese accounting law allows this when the conditions are met, but it brings some specific obligations. This article, compiled by the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam, helps you assess whether to make the switch.

1. What is a foreign accounting currency?

The accounting currency (đơn vị tiền tệ trong kế toán) is the currency used to record all transactions in the books and to prepare the financial statements. The default is the Vietnamese dong (VND). A company may use a foreign currency such as USD only when that currency is the primary currency of its business operations.

Two concepts are often confused: the accounting currency (used for day-to-day bookkeeping) and the presentation currency (used when filing reports with State authorities). Even when the books are kept in USD, financial statements filed in Vietnam must still be translated into VND under current regulations.

2. Conditions for choosing USD as the accounting currency

Current regulations do not allow a foreign currency to be chosen by preference. The company must demonstrate that USD is the currency that drives its cash flows and prices. 4. Translating financial statements into VND

A company keeping its books in USD must still file its annual financial statements (with the audit report, as FDI companies require an independent audit) and its tax returns in VND. The exchange rates used to translate each line item follow the current accounting regime; agree them with your auditor in advance.

In practice, three common errors are: using a single rate for all line items even though the regulations distinguish between them; not disclosing the rates used; and VND figures on the corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) finalization return not matching the translated financial statements. FAQ

Can a newly established company keep its books in USD from the start?

Yes, if it can show that USD is the primary currency of its expected operations. Keep the projected cash flow analysis and notify the tax authority under current regulations from the outset.

Can the VAT return be prepared in USD?

No. Tax returns and tax payable are determined in VND, so a company keeping its books in USD must translate and reconcile every period.

Does USD bookkeeping avoid exchange differences?

Not entirely. Exchange differences shift to VND transactions such as payroll, social insurance, taxes and domestic expenses.

Do auditors review the choice of accounting currency?

Yes. Auditors usually examine the evidence the company used to conclude on its primary currency, so the assessment file should be kept complete.
