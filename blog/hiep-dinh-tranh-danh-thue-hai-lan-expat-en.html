Double Tax Treaties in Vietnam: How Expats Can Apply Them

How expats use tax treaties: the employment income article, exemption or credit, the certificate of residence and a document checklist.

A double tax treaty (DTA – hiệp định tránh đánh thuế hai lần) helps expats avoid paying tax in two places on the same salary, but the benefit does not apply automatically. The expat must fall within the right case, have complete documents and follow the prescribed procedure. This article summarizes how to read the employment income article and the document set that the accounting services team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam typically prepares together with FDI companies.

1. What is a double tax treaty?

It is a bilateral agreement between Vietnam and another country or territory that allocates which country may tax each type of income. Vietnam has signed treaties with many countries; the list and text of each treaty are published on the tax authority's portal. Where a treaty and domestic tax law differ, the general principle is that the treaty prevails.

For an expat, the first question is always in which country the expat is tax resident in the tax year. If they are considered resident in both countries at the same time, the treaty contains tie-breaker rules (permanent home, centre of vital interests, nationality...). See also how to determine PIT residence.

2. The articles commonly used for employment income

Most treaties contain an article on income from dependent personal services (usually Article 15 in the OECD model; the article number may differ in each treaty). This article usually allows the country where the work is performed to tax the income, unless all the exemption conditions are met at the same time. You need to read the specific treaty text, because the day count may be based on the calendar year, the fiscal year or a 12-month period.

3. Exemption or credit for tax already paid

A treaty can help an expat in two ways. Exemption in Vietnam: when the expat is not resident in Vietnam and meets the conditions of the dependent personal services article. Credit for tax paid abroad: when the expat is a tax resident of Vietnam with income already taxed in another country; the creditable amount usually cannot exceed the Vietnamese tax calculated on that income.

5. The process during the year

A common mistake: expats assume that "staying under 183 days means exemption". In fact there are two further conditions concerning who pays the salary and the permanent establishment; if the salary is paid or borne by the Vietnamese company, the exemption usually does not apply. FAQ

Who issues the certificate of residence?

The tax authority of the country in which the expat is resident, stating the tax period. Consular legalization requirements should be checked against the regulations in force at the time of filing.

Can an expat who is resident in Vietnam be exempt under a treaty?

Normally a resident expat is taxed in Vietnam on worldwide income. In that case the treaty mainly allows a credit for tax paid abroad, rather than a full exemption.

Can the company file the treaty application on the expat's behalf?

Yes, with a power of attorney and a complete file. The company should agree with the expat on who is responsible for preparing documents at the start of the year.
