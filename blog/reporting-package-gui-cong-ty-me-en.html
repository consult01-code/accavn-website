Group Reporting Package for the Parent: Converting VAS to IFRS

Step by step, building a VAS→IFRS bridge for the reporting package: adjusting entries, variance controls, a template and a timeline.

Every period, an FDI company must send a reporting package to its parent company in the group's format and accounting framework, usually IFRS, while its books in Vietnam are kept under VAS. The most sustainable approach is not to change the books but to build a conversion schedule (bridge) that can be reused every period. This article walks through the steps the way the accounting team at ACCA Consulting Vietnam does it for FDI clients.

1. What a parent-company reporting package contains

It varies by group, but a reporting package typically includes: a trial balance mapped to the group chart of accounts, an income statement and balance sheet in the group format, an intercompany balance reconciliation, notes on material line items, and a VAS-to-IFRS adjustment schedule. Many groups submit through consolidation software, but the underlying logic is the same.

Explaining each adjustment

ADJ-01 arises when a lease is expensed under VAS but IFRS requires a right-of-use asset and lease liability. ADJ-02 reflects the difference between an aging-based allowance and the expected credit loss model. ADJ-03 is common when bonuses are recorded only when paid, whereas IFRS requires recognition when the obligation arises. ADJ-04 separates the value of the bundled warranty service so that the revenue is recognized over time.

ADJ-05 is the step most often forgotten: each adjustment reduces accounting profit but does not change the tax payable in Vietnam, so it creates a temporary difference. At the standard corporate income tax (CIT/TNDN) rate of 20%, total adjustments of −453 million create a deferred tax asset of about 91 million. The rate for a particular company may differ if it falls under the 15% or 17% rates or enjoys incentives, so use the rate expected to apply when the difference reverses.

FAQ

Should IFRS entries be posted directly into the VAS books?

No. The VAS books serve the financial statements and tax in Vietnam; IFRS adjustments should be recorded in a separate ledger or bridge so every figure remains traceable.

Does the reporting package need to be audited?

It depends on the group's requirements. Many groups ask local auditors to perform procedures on the year-end package, separate from the statutory VAS audit.

Does the bridge have to be rebuilt every month?

No. Prior-period adjustments become opening balances, and each month only the movements are updated. That is why a stable template saves a lot of time.

Does a small company need a complex reporting package?

It depends on the group's requirements. Small companies often need only a mapped trial balance and a few material adjustments.
