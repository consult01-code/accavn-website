First-Year Audit for FDI Companies in Vietnam: Checklist

A checklist of documents specific to an FDI company's first-year audit, and the mistakes that most often lead to a qualified audit opinion.

An FDI company's first-year audit differs from later years in one key respect: every balance is "built from zero", and many transactions take place before the company even has a seal or a bank account. If documents are missing for these early transactions, the auditor may issue a qualified opinion on the very first financial statements sent to the parent company. 2. Capital contribution records and the 90-day deadline

A foreign investor's capital contribution must be transferred through the direct investment capital account (DICA – tài khoản vốn đầu tư trực tiếp) within 90 days of the Enterprise Registration Certificate (ERC – Giấy chứng nhận đăng ký doanh nghiệp) issue date. The auditor will compare each inflow against this deadline and against the capital stated on the ERC. The risks of late contribution are covered in capital contribution deadlines for foreign investors.

  • DICA bank statements show the remitter's name matching the investor's name on the IRC, with the description clearly stating "capital contribution".
  • Foreign currency contributions are recorded at the actual exchange rate on the date of receipt; keep the calculation.
  • For in-kind contributions: valuation minutes, handover minutes and documents transferring ownership.

3. Pre-incorporation costs and imported assets

Market surveys, consultants' fees, flights, temporary office rental and similar costs are often paid by the parent company or the individual investor before the legal entity exists. To record them in the new company's books, you need a cost transfer agreement, a detailed list and the original documents. Without these, the auditor will struggle to accept the balances and the tax authority will struggle to accept them as deductible expenses.

For imported machinery, historical cost includes the purchase price, non-refundable taxes, transport, installation and testing costs. A common mistake is expensing installation costs in the period instead of capitalizing them, or lacking a handover minute putting the asset into use so that depreciation can begin.

FAQ

Does an FDI company established late in the year need an audit for that year?

The first accounting period is determined under current regulations on accounting periods; where the first period is short, it may be combined with the following year. Clarify this early with your accountant and audit firm.

Can pre-incorporation costs paid by the parent company be recorded as expenses?

They can, if there is a cost transfer agreement, valid original documents, and the expenses serve the company's operations. Related-party transaction rules and the associated tax implications also need to be considered.

What is the impact of a qualified opinion in the first year?

The parent company and banks will read the financial statements together with this opinion; in addition, unresolved issues may carry over into the following year's opinion.
